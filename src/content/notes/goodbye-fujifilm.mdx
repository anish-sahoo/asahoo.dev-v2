*tl;dr - I loved my Fuji X-T4, but over time it started to have shutter issues. So I sold everything and switched to a Sony A7v.*

I've had the Fujifilm X-T4 for almost 5 years. Since buying it in mid-2021, it has seen countless cities and landscapes and hundreds of thousands of shutter actuations. It is an amazing tool, has a lot of pros and very few cons.

I had a whole lot of Fuji gear: *the X-T4 camera*, *18-55 f/2.8-4*, *33mm f/1.4*, *23mm f/2*, *rokinon 12mm f/2*. At some point, I had considered switching over to the X100VI, but then cancelled my pre-order after about 6 months of waiting for it to ship.

## Things I loved/hated:
I usually shoot street photography, and rarely portraits or landscapes (only if I'm out with friends or visiting places). I really liked the compact form factor of Fujifilm cameras/lenses, and the lenses are very solid despite being really small. 

A lot of fuji lenses have a lot of *character* (certain imperfections and sharpness/depth-of-field rolloff that attracts the eye), and pair really well with the film-like color science and recipes that this camera has. I've gotten some of my favorite shots SOOC using *Classic Chrome* or recipes like *Kodak Gold 200*. 

I learned about my love for photographing rainy days through using this camera. I've spent a lot of time photographing NYC and Boston in the rain. Sometimes with an umbrella, but often without. This camera, paired with WR lenses, has casually survived pouring rains and storms without breaking a sweat. After reflecting on all the conditions I've used this camera in, I realized that weather sealing will be one of my most important requirements for any future camera.

## Why did I switch systems?
This camera was very dependable, but it was starting to age. I started getting `'Please turn off camera and turn on again'` errors. The issue was a loose mount, which would  randomly disconnect the electrical contacts. This was a natural outcome after having used this camera in rain and dust and snow for so many years, and having switched lenses often in these conditions.

Repairs would've costed me >$500, and I didn't want to invest any more than I had already into this system. *I'm still in college, after all.* But I've saved up a decent amount of money from internships, so I figured it's time I sunset the Fuji, and switch to full-frame. 

So I did what I've been thinking about doing for a while -- I sold all the fuji gear to a local camera store, and bought a Sony A7v with the 24-50 f/2.8 G lens.

## Why not just replace the body instead?
I considered this, but I don't particularly like any of the modern Fuji bodies. 

The X-T5 is known to have inferior build quality compared to the X-T4. I don't like the XH and XS series because they have PSAM dials. If I'll be on Fuji, it has to be a camera that looks and works like old-school film cameras. I require weather sealing, that removes 70% of their offerings.

There's also inventory issues, as I mentioned earlier. I cancelled my X100VI pre-order after I ran out of patience. This is very common now given the scalpers and popularity of Fujifilm across social media. I didn't want to deal with this.

Final reason, it was time for a change. I have used Fujifilm for such a long time that I practically have the settings and stuff memorized. I belive that interacting with the camera is part of the experience, and I was getting bored with what I have.

## What now?
I'm learning to use the Sony system! The A7v outperforms my old X-T4 in every possible way (except colors). Imagine my surprise when I upload a photo to instagram without any special settings, and it looks visibly sharper than my older photos. 

They did something with the color science on the Sony A7v and A7Rvi, it is much better than other Sony cameras. There's also this `Creative Looks` feature that brings the colors 80-90% of the way to Fuji colors. I'm pleasantly surprised that I am able to use a lot of Sony photos SOOC. The lens is also pretty good, I'm just getting used to the slightly bulkier setup. The low light performance is crazy, I can use ISO 25600 and still get usable photos. The Fuji would start choking at ISO 4000.

Overall, I'm enjoying my new camera system!
